**(1) `LARGE_HUNTER_MAX = 100` IS DELETED AND THE CEILING SCALES.** Its own comment called it "a runaway backstop that play should never reach" — and play never did, which is precisely the defect. `largeHunterCap(wave) = min(ceil(wave / DEBUG.hunterCapLevelsPerStep), DEBUG.hunterCapMax)` — **1 large Hunter at levels 1–2, one more every two levels, plateauing at 6 from level 11.** Two new HUNTER-section knobs: **`hunterCapMax`** (def 6, 1–24) and **`hunterCapLevelsPerStep`** (def 2, 1–8). **⛔ NOT A LEVER AND MUST NEVER BECOME ONE** — no floor/ceil/steps triple, no `▼`/`↳`, no `carriesTo`, no `LEVERS` entry: a ceiling on concurrent threats is a **stability guarantee, not a difficulty axis**, and the player should never feel it move. **(2) THE OVERFLOW RULE REVERSES AGAIN — THREE ARMS, NOT TWO — AND P3's REASONING IS REWRITTEN IN PLACE RATHER THAN DELETED.** P3 made a saturated clump **destroy itself** at a full cap, arguing that with decay gone a held clump would occupy 12 pieces the pipeline could never reclaim. **⛔ THAT ARGUMENT IS WRONG, AND EXACTLY WHERE IT FAILS IS THE VALUABLE PART: it assumed a held clump has no reclamation path. It has two, and both are the player's — a held clump is still garbage, so it can be SCOOPED (partially or wholly) and SHATTERED with a bullet.** Scooping a 12-piece clump for a single large payload is the game's own thesis paying out, and is the removed bonus canister's role arriving emergently instead of by dice roll. New rule: **cap has room → convert** (unchanged); **cap full and fewer than `DEBUG.heldClumpMax` saturated clumps → HOLD** (no `hunterCoalesced`, no `noteLargeHunterSpawn()`, no `AudioSys.hunterborn()` — nothing was born); **cap full AND `heldClumpMax` already held → destroy exactly as P3 does**, `boom()` in the garbage hue, `awardScore = false` semantics. New knob **`heldClumpMax`** (def 4, 1–20) is the anti-stall backstop, sized against `GARBAGE_SOFT_MAX` (220): unbounded holding would let ~18 clumps consume the whole garbage budget and starve the field.

**(3) ⛔ "HELD" IS A DERIVED CONDITION, NEVER A STORED FLAG — `saturatedClump(g)` is `!g.dead && g.pieces >= HUNTER_COALESCE_COUNT`, and it has THREE consumers reading the ONE condition** (the conversion branch, the cull exemption, the visual tell). Two shipped paths change a clump's size *after* it saturates and a flag would survive both: **scooping is PARTIAL** (`take = Math.min(room, g.pieces)`, so a held 12-clump met with 5 free chain slots gives up 5 and survives at 7) and **a bullet SHATTERS** any `pieces > 1` clump. Either would leave a 7-piece clump still marked as a pending Hunter. Derived, there is no state to clear and no way to desync — pinned directly, including a partial scoop through the real intake and a shatter, both proving the clump behaves as ordinary salvage on the very same frame.

**(4) A SATURATED CLUMP STOPS ABSORBING — the pair is skipped on BOTH sides in `coalesceGarbage()`, attraction AND merge.** Otherwise a held clump grows into a 40-piece blob that still converts to exactly one Hunter and wastes 28 pieces. **The consequence is accepted and is not a bug: loose singles near a held clump sit there rather than merging into it** — even one sitting exactly on top of it. They are still scoopable and still shootable.

**(5) `drainHeldClumps()` — THE QUEUE DRAIN, a new end-of-frame pass in `update()`'s cleanup block.** `coalesceGarbage()`'s pair walk only touches clumps near each other, so a held clump sitting alone is never re-examined and would hold forever after a Hunter died. The drain converts the **oldest by `age`** (ties by array index, strict `>`), repeating while slots remain — **it can convert several in one frame**, because one event can free several slots at once. **Conversion through it is a REAL birth**: `noteLargeHunterSpawn()`, `hunterCoalesced++`, `hunterborn()`, all of it. It sits **before** `cullGarbage()` and **after** every pass that can mark a Hunter dead — both directions load-bearing (a large destroyed this frame frees its slot immediately, since `largeHunterCount()` excludes dead-but-unfiltered hunters). `noteLargeHunterSpawn()` therefore has **two** callers now, not one — the same producer at two moments — and every birth still funnels through the one 0→1 helper.

**⛔ ONE DECISION THE PHASE PROMPT DID NOT NAME, MADE DELIBERATELY AND FLAGGED HERE: `drainHeldClumps()` HONOURS `game.sweepPause`.** CS018 P9 set that pause specifically because a Super Mega Delivery turns every large into mediums, freeing the **whole** ceiling at once, and fresh larges converting into the fragment swarm is exactly what it exists to prevent. **The cull's design, and the two things about it that are decisions rather than details.** It is **fully deterministic** — same inputs, same victims, every run. Frame-rate-reactive culling was considered and rejected for the three reasons the prompt gives, and the code carries that rejection as a comment so nobody reintroduces it on a later pass. **Ordering (FLAG-CS024-e, invented here, no precedent in the build): singles before clumps, then oldest first, array order breaking an exact tie.** Singles first because a clump is player-relevant progress — worth more per hook, and a lineage on its way to becoming a Hunter — while a stray single is the cheapest thing on the board to lose. **`age` never resets, not even on a merge**, which is the exact inverse of the retired decay clock's rule: decay reset on merge so a growing lineage would never die, but the cull wants the opposite answer — a clump that has been sitting around is precisely what it should prefer to take. **Placement is load-bearing twice over:** `cullGarbage()` is the first line of `update()`'s `--- Cleanup ---` block, which puts it after every pass that can mark a piece dead (so the excess is measured against pieces genuinely still in the world) and immediately before the garbage dead-filter (so a culled piece is swept by the SAME one filter as every other death and can never survive a frame to be drawn). It is **silent** — proven, not asserted: a recording ctx and a wrapped `AudioSys.explosion` confirm zero particles, zero floaters, zero sound across four consecutive culls. **Deliberately NOT mirrored into `updateDeath()`'s filter pass** — that sim runs no coalescence so it pays no O(n²) cost, and the run is over inside `DEATH_DURATION`; culling there would only delete salvage during the death spectacle.

**Two code paths, one ordering definition.** The soft cull (the common case, running every frame while the field is saturated) is a single O(n) linear scan with no allocation; the hard backstop sorts once, O(n log n), rather than rescanning n times — so a pathological count cannot turn the drain itself into the stall it exists to prevent. Both call the same `betterCullVictim(a, b)` predicate, and the sort comparator calls it **in both directions** plus an explicit `i - j` index tiebreak, so the two paths agree by construction and the result never depends on whether the engine's sort is stable. `cullGarbage()` is also written so an **inverted knob pair** (soft > hard) yields a negative count and culls nothing rather than misbehaving — which is what makes it safe that no registry entry has ever validated against a sibling.

## Balance notes

- **CS024 P7 — ENGINE IS NOW 10 SECONDS OF THRUST, AND IT IS THE ONLY NUMBER GATE B MOVED.** `ENGINE_BURN_SECONDS` 5.0 → **10.0**, per Q11. The unit is unchanged and is the thing to keep in mind when reading it: **seconds of FORWARD THRUST, not wall time** — the tank drains only on frames where thrust is actually applied, so 10 s of fuel is far more than 10 s of powerup in practice, and a player who coasts keeps it indefinitely. **⛔ THE BRIEFING — WHAT CHANGED UNDER THIS GATE IN CS024 P6c, BECAUSE THE PANEL YOU WILL OPEN IS NOT THE ONE THIS GATE WAS WRITTEN FOR.** The previous version of this block described **one flat slider per lever**, showing `auto`, which pinned that lever to a constant the moment you touched it. **That is gone.** A lever is a floor, a ceiling and a step count, so it now has **three rows each — 51 in all** — and moving any one of them re-derives that lever's whole ramp immediately, at every level.

**The three rows, and what each one actually does:**

- **`· floor`** — the value the lever holds at **level 1**, and the value it returns to **exactly** on every wrap. Move this to change where the ramp *starts*.

For a **driver** this is its **period**: `junkCount` at 10 steps wraps every 10 levels, at 7 it wraps every 7. **Four things about that table worth knowing BEFORE you play, because each one will otherwise look like a bug.** **(1) THE SATURATION COLUMN IS WHY THE PLAY RANGE MOVED TO 45.** CS024 P6b restaged the nine UFO dependents so they saturate in a deliberate ORDER — speed (L33), then rate of fire (L41), then evasiveness (L49), then shot velocity (L57), and **accuracy last (L65)** — the UFOs get *faster* before they get *accurate*.
