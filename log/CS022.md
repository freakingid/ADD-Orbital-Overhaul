**THE CHAIN'S FLOAT CONTRACT, STATED HONESTLY RATHER THAN OVERCLAIMED.** The phase prompt and spec §8 item 5 ask for chain-node `x − px` / `y − py` to be **bit-identical** across a resize. `resizeWorld` shifts `x` and `px` by the *identical double*, which is the correct implementation — but bit-identity is not a property IEEE-754 provides: both additions have to be individually exact, and they are not always. A 2M-sample fuzz over realistic coordinates (positions 0–5120, chain offsets ≤ 480 px, per-frame velocities ≤ 9 px) puts the miss rate near **a third** and the worst deviation at **2.274e-13 px** — one half-ulp at world-coordinate magnitude, i.e. ~1e-11 px/s of implied velocity. The alternative formulation (capture `v`, set `x`, rebuild `px = x − v`) was fuzzed head-to-head and is **exactly as inexact**, so there is no better implementation to switch to. The suite therefore asserts a **hard, documented ceiling of 1e-12 px** on every node in every driven transition, AND proves `===` on a case built from exactly-representable coordinates (where any scaling or clamping of the chain breaks it outright), AND carries a control showing that moving `x` without `px` changes the implied velocity by ~256 px/frame. Asserting `===` universally would have shipped a flaky test.

**BULLETS ARE DELIBERATELY NOT RE-HOMED, and this is the one risk the phase prompt did not flag.** Spec §4.2 enumerates the seven carried arrays and `game.bullets` is not among them; the spec was followed rather than silently widened. Practical effect: a bullet in flight across a transition keeps its old absolute coordinates. On a grow it is simply somewhere else relative to the player and dies on its own life timer; on a shrink `wrap()` folds it on the next frame (one period is always enough here, since 5120 < 2×2560 + 60). Nothing is lost or duplicated and nothing measures from a bullet. Flagged because it is a real, if cosmetic, behaviour and a future phase may want it in the list.
