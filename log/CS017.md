## Balance notes

- **CS017 P5 bonus canister — four first-pass PLAYTEST KNOBS, browser feel entirely unverified.** `BONUS_CANISTER_PIECES` (6 chain slots), `BONUS_CANISTER_SCORE` (250 points), `BONUS_SPAWN_CHANCE_EARLY` (0.5) and `BONUS_SPAWN_CHANCE_LATE` (0.1). Shipped curve, measured from the real code: **50%, 45%, 40%, 35%, 30%, 25%, 20%, 15%, 10%** across a cycle, resetting to 50% at every boundary — so a player sees roughly **2.7 bonus canisters per 9-wave cycle** on average, front-loaded. For scale, 250 points is a little over a **five**-canister dock delivery (`50+75+100+125+150` = 500 for five, so 250 ≈ two-and-a-bit canisters' worth) — it is a nudge, not a jackpot, and the *real* reward is the six free chain slots it fills, which then have to be towed somewhere. **The interaction to watch is with the tow cap**, not with score: six nodes is half of `CARGO_BASE` (12), so an early-wave scoop takes the player from empty to half-loaded in one pickup, with the full mass penalty that implies (~54%/70% thrust/top-speed at a base-12 chain, §2.10). That is the intended "invites greed" bite. If it reads as a punishment rather than a temptation, lower `BONUS_CANISTER_PIECES` before touching anything else. **The spawn chance is the one lever in CS017 that eases off** — it is registered in `DIFFICULTY-LEVERS.md` §2.7 as such, and §2.6 records why it is the one lever that interpolates linearly rather than on the shipped `ramp()` curve (the constants are cycle endpoints; `difficultyFactor` never reaches them in 9 waves).

- **CS017 P4 saucer pressure axis — three first-pass PLAYTEST KNOBS, browser feel entirely unverified, and landed AHEAD of the P3 playtest gate below (Paul's explicit call to proceed).** `saucerPressureSecs` (90s to reach full pressure), `saucerAimPressure` (0.5), `saucerGapPressure` (0.5) are all dev-tunable via the hidden Debug Options panel, not hardcoded — this is the escape hatch for retuning. At the default 0.5 scale, lingering a full 90s in a level only closes HALF the remaining gap to each lever's `_CEIL` (`base + (CEIL-base) × 1 × 0.5`), not the full gap — raise the scale toward 1 for a harder squeeze, or raise `saucerPressureSecs` to make the pressure build more slowly. Open questions for a browser session: (a) is 90s the right horizon for "the level is dragging on," given wave lengths vary a lot with debris count/Hunter presence? (b) does the tightened aim/gap read as legible pressure ("the saucer's getting serious") or as an invisible, only-numbers-show-it change? (c) does it meaningfully counteract farming, or does a competent player clear before pressure ever meaningfully builds? (d) how does it interact with the P5 bonus canister's incentive to linger (deliberately named in-spec as a design tension, PLANNED-FEATURES-CS017.md FORK-CS017-B) — does one incentive dominate the other in practice? **Because P4 landed without the P3 browser playtest gate being cleared first (see Next up), a browser session evaluating either should probably evaluate BOTH the sawtooth reset feel (P3) and the pressure axis (P4) together, since they're both live and both first-pass.**

- **CS017 P3 sawtooth escalation — six first-pass PLAYTEST KNOBS, browser feel entirely unverified.** `CYCLE_LENGTH` (9) and `CYCLE_GAIN` (0.12) from P1 are now live for the first time, joined by `DEBRIS_COUNT_MAX` (12), `DEBRIS_COUNT_HARD_MAX` (24), `DEBRIS_SPEED_PER_WAVE` (0.08, the old inline value, so not a retune) and `DEBRIS_SPEED_CAP` (1040). Shipped curve, measured from the real code: debris count opens **4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 8, 8** per cycle and tops out **12, 13, 15, 16, 18, 19, 21, 22, 24, 24**; the speed multiplier runs 1.00→1.64, then 1.12→1.84, then 1.24→2.03. **Waves 1–6 are byte-identical to the pre-P3 build, and the whole of cycle 0 is unchanged for speed and Hunters** — the Pillar-6 ease-in is preserved exactly; the first divergence a player meets is a fuller field at waves 7–9 (10/11/12 pieces vs the old cap of 9). The question for a browser session is whether the wave-10 reset **reads as relief** or as the game going soft, and whether 12→13→15 across cycle tops is enough escalation to feel like progress.

- **CS017 P3 — the integer-rounding quantization at the cycle opening (surfaced deliberately, not hidden).** The count is `Math.round`ed, and at `CYCLE_GAIN` 0.12 the opening base of 4 needs **two** cycles to move a whole piece: `round(4 × 1.12) = 4`. So cycle 1's opening wave spawns the same 4 debris as cycle 0's, and the spiral is invisible *at the opening wave* until cycle 2 — it is plainly visible at every other point in the cycle from cycle 1 onward, since the cycle top moves every single cycle. This is a consequence of the specified formula, not a bug; the fix if a playtest dislikes it is to raise `CYCLE_GAIN` (or raise the opening base). The headless test asserts this as a documented fact from the real helper, so a retune that changes it trips the suite rather than sliding silently.

- **CS017 P3 — `DEBRIS_SPEED_CAP` ships inert BY DESIGN (FLAG-CS017-a, resolved as a guard rail).** Measured in the headless negative control: the fastest debris ever constructed over waves 1–30 is **≈423 px/s** against the 1040 cap, and the cap only starts to engage around cycle 18 (~wave 171). Nothing in normal play touches it. It exists so a future `CYCLE_GAIN`/`DEBRIS_SPEED_PER_WAVE` retune cannot produce unhittable debris. Do not delete it on a cleanup pass because "it never fires."

- **CS017 P3 — a Hunter carried across a cycle boundary stays fast (accepted, not a bug).** Speed and turn rate are sampled once at spawn. Since CS015 P3 Hunters do not block wave-clear, so a Hunter born late in one cycle survives into the next one still moving at its spawn cycle's values while the debris field around it has reset. Paul's explicit call at P3 was to accept this; it is worth watching in a browser session, because it is the one place where the "reset = relief" promise can be undercut.

- **CS017 P3 — a new footgun for future phases and tests: `game.wave = N` alone no longer sets difficulty.** The sawtooth levers read `game.cycle`/`game.cycleWave`, and **only `nextWave()` writes them**. Any test (or future code) that pre-positions the wave counter must drive the real `nextWave()` rather than assigning `game.wave` — `test-f5.js` was already doing the latter and silently produced wave-1 difficulty at "wave 20" until it was repointed this session. The idiom now used across the suite is `game.wave = w - 1; game.debris.length = 0; nextWave();`.
